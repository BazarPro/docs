In the basement there's the kids' bike your child outgrew long ago, the neighbor's old Dutch bike is rusting away – and at the same time, half the town is looking for a cheap used bike. A **bike sale** – also called a bike exchange, bike market or bike swap – brings both together: sellers drop off their bikes, a team sells them in one day, and at the end everyone gets their money or their bike back.

Sounds simple – and it is, if it's well prepared. In this guide we walk through a bike sale from the first idea to the cash count. And we show how [BazarPro](https://bazarpro.de) takes lists, handwritten notes and counting in the evening off your hands.

<Aside type="tip" title="In short">
  Settle venue and date early, advertise widely, set clear time slots for drop-off, sale and pickup – and every bike gets a QR label. The rest is scanning.
</Aside>

## Part 1: Planning

### Settle venue and date early

Take care of the **venue** first – good spaces are often booked months ahead. A schoolyard, a parking lot, club grounds or the market square work well. Look out for:

- **Enough space:** With spacing, a bike needs a good 1.5 m². For 150 bikes, plan at least 250–300 m², plus paths, intake and cash desk.
- **A site you can close off.** The bikes stand outside all day. The site should be **fenced off** – with construction fencing, barrier tape or crowd barriers – and have only one entrance and exit, where the cash desk is.
- **Power and shelter** for intake and cash desk: a gazebo, a table, a socket for the printer and chargers.
- **Date:** Spring (March to May) is peak season, when many people are looking for a bike. Check whether other events take place nearby on the same day.
- **Permit:** On public spaces like the market square you usually need a permit from the town. Ask the local authorities early. On school or club grounds, the owner's consent is usually enough.
- **Insurance:** Check whether your club's or school's liability insurance covers the event.
- **Plan B for rain:** A covered alternative or enough gazebos. Bikes can handle rain, paper and electronics less so.

### Set three time slots

A bike sale has three phases that you should keep clearly apart:

1. **Drop-off** – e.g. Saturday 8:00–10:00: sellers bring their bikes.
2. **Sale** – e.g. 10:00–14:00: the site is open to buyers.
3. **Pickup and payout** – e.g. 15:00–16:00: sellers collect their money or their unsold bikes.

Between sale and pickup you need some buffer to get an overview. Enter drop-off and pickup in BazarPro in the **Hand-in and pickup** step and put the times in all your advertising – it saves you many questions on the day. BazarPro then sends your sellers an email the evening before with the drop-off time, location and seller number, and reminds them to pick up after the sale.

### Create the bazaar in BazarPro

When you create the bazaar, you first pick its **kind**. There are two presets for a bike sale:

- **Bike exchange with cash desk** – the classic case: bikes are dropped off, sold at a cash desk, and paid out afterwards. With photos, negotiable prices and price offers.
- **Bike market with record** – for markets that must show who offered which bike (see below).

The preset sets suitable options and preselects the bike categories. Then you add the key details: title, location, date, a description with the time slots, the allowed categories (e.g. bikes, kids' bikes, accessories) and your **commission** – the share of the sale price that stays with you as the organizer. With 15 % commission and a bike for €40, the seller gets €34 and €6 stays with you.

Many bike sales charge an **intake fee** in addition to or instead of the commission, for example €3 per bike. Enter it in the **What does it cost sellers?** step. It applies to every accepted bike, even if it doesn't sell, and is deducted from the payout at settlement. Sellers who sell nothing pay the fee at pickup. BazarPro then shows you how much you get.

Optionally, you can upload a **site map** and add locations like “Kids' bikes”, “Women's bikes” or “E-bikes”. Buyers then see where a bike stands when they scan it.

For categories whose name contains “Fahrrad”, “Räder”, “Rad” or “bike”, such as “Kinderräder” or “E-Bikes”, BazarPro asks for the **frame number, frame size, wheel size and brand** when a bike is created. Under **Required details** you make the frame number mandatory – then a bike can't be registered without it.

To make sure the space is enough, you can limit the **items per seller**, for example to 5 bikes.

The step-by-step guide is in [Create a bazaar](/en/veranstalter/basar-anlegen/).

### Choose features for your bazaar

In the **How are items sold?** step you decide how items are sold. For a bike sale, price offers, negotiating and questions are especially worthwhile. Discounts are possible too – but then there's no price on the label:

| Feature | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Discount items** | Sellers lower their price during the bazaar. You decide from what time, e.g. only from 12:00, and how high the discount may be at most (up to 50 %). |
| **Online price offers** | Logged-in buyers suggest a price by phone once a bike has been accepted and the bazaar is running. If the seller accepts, that price applies at the cash desk. |
| **Negotiating on site (minimum price)** | Sellers set a minimum price that buyers don't see. At the cash desk you may negotiate down to that price. |
| **Questions to the seller** | Buyers ask publicly even before the bazaar, e.g. “Is the light included?”. Everyone sees the answer. With **Only during the bazaar**, this is only possible once the sale starts. |

Payment always happens on site at the cash desk.

### Bike market with a record for the police

Some bike markets have to show – for example at the request of the police – who offered which bike. That's what the **Bike market with record** preset is for:

- **Private sellers only:** sellers confirm at registration that they don't sell commercially.
- **Frame number required** and at most 5 bikes per seller.
- **Fixed data:** from the first registered bike, the seller's name and address and the bike's title, price and frame number are fixed. Withdrawing is only possible until the market starts.
- **Record as PDF or CSV:** on the dashboard you download a list of all offered bikes with seller, address, frame number and sale.

More in [The bazaar day](/en/veranstalter/basartag/#record-of-offered-items).

### Set conditions for sellers

Put the most important rules in the bazaar description so there are no discussions on the day:

- **Commission** and time slots for drop-off and pickup.
- **Sold as seen:** between private persons, warranty can be excluded. Tell buyers to check the bike before buying.
- **Bikes not picked up:** what happens to them after a deadline, for example a donation to a bike workshop?

### More than bikes: food and drinks

A **food and drinks stand** turns the sale into a small party. Buyers stay longer, get talking and are more likely to buy. For clubs, daycares or school classes, selling coffee, cake and sausages is also a welcome income.

## Part 2: Sellers get ready

Sellers register for the bazaar through the link and create their bikes from home – with photo, description and price. For each bike, BazarPro creates a **QR label** as a PDF. The label is printed and attached to the bike, ideally to the handlebar.

The label shows the QR code, title and **seller number**. Whether the **price** is on it too is up to you: with **Print the price on the label**, buyers see it without a phone – but only if you don't allow discounts. With discounts, the price is left off so that sellers can still lower it without printing a new label.

To make the listing convincing, sellers should:

- enter the **frame number**. It helps against suspicion of theft and belongs on the purchase contract.
- give frame and wheel size, because that's what buyers look for first.
- set a **minimum price** if you allow negotiating on site. Buyers then see “Price negotiable” but not the minimum price.
- answer buyers' **questions** quickly. BazarPro sends an email and marks “My items” in the menu.

The guide for sellers: [List an item](/en/verkaeufer/artikel-inserieren/).

If something changes at short notice, such as the entrance or the drop-off time, write to all sellers with **Message to sellers** – by email and on their bazaar page.

<Aside type="tip" title="Tip for sellers">
  Put labels in a **waterproof sleeve** – a small plastic sleeve or a freezer bag with a cable tie will do. Then the QR code survives a rain shower.
</Aside>

## Part 3: The bazaar day

### What to have ready

- **Smartphones or tablets** for intake and cash desk – BazarPro runs in the browser, no till needed. You can also connect a **hand scanner** at the cash desk. Don't forget power banks.
- **A printer** with paper for sellers who forgot their label.
- **Waterproof sleeves and cable ties** for labels in case it rains.
- **Change** and a lockable cash box.
- **Fencing material** for the site.
- Optional: a **long cable lock** or racks to lock up e-bikes (see below).
- Optional: **purchase contracts** on paper for buyers who want a signed contract.

### Assign helpers

  <figcaption style="margin-top: 0.5rem; text-align: center;">This is what a well-organized bike sale looks like on site</figcaption>

You can hardly run a bike sale alone. In BazarPro you make sellers of your bazaar **helpers** with one click. Helpers can:

- handle **intake** and scan bikes,
- sell at the **cash desk**,
- handle **settlement and returns**,
- print **QR codes and invoices**.

Helpers can't change the basic settings or delete the bazaar – that stays with the organizer. Plan at least one person per station, two at the cash desk at peak times.

<Aside type="tip" title="Bike marking and safety check">
  Many bike sales offer **bike marking** against theft or a short **road safety check**, often together with a cycling club or the police. Ask your local group. Such a stand attracts additional visitors.
</Aside>

### Intake: scan every bike

<Steps>

1. **Take the bike and scan its label.** In Item management, open **Scan QR code** and scan the label. The bike is marked as **Available** and is now for sale. It is locked for the seller – they can no longer edit it.

2. **Location and note (optional).** If you created locations, assign the bike to an area right away, e.g. “Kids' bikes”. In the same step you can add an **internal note**, such as “Key on hook 12”. Only you and your helpers see the note.

3. **No label?** No problem: every seller has a **digital ID** with a QR code on their phone in BazarPro. Scan the ID – BazarPro shows all items of that seller. Simply print the missing label at the intake printer.

</Steps>

### Walk-in sellers without an account

Not everyone registers beforehand – some just turn up at intake with their bike. For them, you create an entry in the seller management with **Add seller without account**, with name and phone number and optionally an email address. In Item management, **Add item** records the bike for this seller; you print the label and accept the bike like any other. If the seller gave an email address, they get a message like everyone else as soon as their bike sells.

<Aside title="Parking e-bikes safely">
  E-bikes are valuable. A proven approach: the seller brings their **own lock** and locks the bike to a fixed rack. They hand in the key at intake, you hang it on a numbered key board and enter the number as an **internal note** on the bike. When the bike sells, the item list at the cash desk shows which key belongs to it – and the lock goes back to the seller, or they leave it to the buyer.
</Aside>

### The sale: scan instead of search

From the start of the sale, the site is open to buyers.

- **Buyers inform themselves.** They scan the QR code on the bike with their phone camera and immediately see photos, description, condition, current price and location – without an app, and at public bazaars without logging in.
- **Sellers can give discounts.** If you allow discounts, sellers lower the price of their bikes from the set time, at most down to your maximum discount. Anyone who hasn't sold their bike by noon can make it more attractive this way. No new label is needed because the price isn't on it.
- **Buyers make price offers.** With price offers turned on, a logged-in buyer offers a price by phone right at the bike. The seller gets an email and accepts or declines, even from home.
- **The cash desk scans.** Once a buyer has decided, they push the bike to the cash desk. There a helper scans the label on the **Cash desk** page – if someone buys a bike with a helmet and a lock, everything lands in one cart. For each bike, the helper chooses the price: the label price, an accepted price offer or – if negotiable – a negotiated price that may not be below the minimum price. **Complete sale** sells everything. The seller gets an email and sees the sale under “My sales”.
- **Receipt on request.** If the buyer gives their email address at the cash desk, they get a **receipt by email**. Alternatively, you download a **private invoice** as a PDF and print it.

<Aside type="caution" title="Only one exit">
  Let bikes leave the site only through the cash desk. With a single exit right at the cash desk, no bike leaves unscanned.
</Aside>

### Pickup and payout

After the sale, the sellers come back – BazarPro has reminded them of the pickup by email, with the payout amount and a list of their unsold bikes. They show their **digital ID**, you scan it and see at a glance what sold and which bikes are still there.

<Steps>

1. **Return unsold bikes.** BazarPro lists the bikes still on site. The seller takes them along.

2. **Pay out.** BazarPro has already calculated the sales minus your commission and any intake fee. You pay out the amount. If the sales don't cover the intake fee, you collect the difference instead.

3. **Finish the settlement.** One click confirms both. The seller is then settled: sales and returns can no longer be changed.

</Steps>

<Aside type="caution" title="Undoing a settlement">
  If something is wrong after all, undo the settlement. Take the paid-out money back from the seller – BazarPro asks about this so that the cash adds up. Then you can correct and settle again.
</Aside>

## Part 4: After the bazaar

### Sort out bikes left behind

Not everyone comes to pickup on time. In Item management you see which bikes are still **Available**, i.e. neither sold nor picked up. With **Message to sellers** and the choice **Only sellers with items still to pick up**, you reach everyone concerned at once. You find each seller's **phone number** in the seller management – give them a quick call to clarify whether the bike will still be collected or what should happen to it. The conditions you set beforehand help here, such as donating bikes not picked up after a deadline.

Once all bikes are sorted out, set the rest to **Returned** with one click.

### Cash count

Finally, open the **Cash count**. There you see the day's sales, your commission and the intake fees as income, what was paid out to sellers and which payouts are still open – and you can compare it with the cash in the till. No counting slips, no calculator.
