Kids grow out of their clothes faster than they can wear them out. A **kids' bazaar** – also called a children's clothing sale, consignment sale or number bazaar – gives clothes, toys, books and gear a second life: parents drop off their things, a team sells them in one day, and at the end everyone gets their money and the unsold items back.

Unlike a [bike sale](/en/ratgeber/fahrradboerse-organisieren/), this isn't about a few dozen bikes but about **hundreds or thousands of small items**. Most of the work is therefore in accepting, sorting and returning – and that's exactly where [BazarPro](https://bazarpro.de) helps: every item has a QR code, and at the end the system knows what belongs to whom and how much money everyone gets.

<Aside title="Also for adult clothing bazaars">
  Everything in this guide works just as well for a **clothing bazaar for adults**, such as a women's clothing sale: many items, sizes, small labels and a fast cash desk.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="In short">
  Start early, limit the number of sellers, set clear rules for items and labels, plan enough helpers for intake and sorting – and every item gets a QR label.
</Aside>

## Part 1: Planning

### Find a team

A kids' bazaar is teamwork. Usually a daycare, a school, a parents' association, a playgroup or the parish is behind it. A small **organizing team** of three to five people plans; on the bazaar day you need many more hands – for intake, sorting, cash desk, café and returns.

A proven incentive for helpers: anyone who helps may **shop before the official opening**.

### Room and date

- **Room:** A gym, a parish hall or the school hall. You need lots of table space, clothes racks for jackets and dresses and a separate area for intake.
- **Date:** Many bazaars take place twice a year – in spring for summer clothes, in autumn for winter clothes. Check whether there's already a bazaar nearby on the same day.
- **Power and internet:** Sockets for chargers and the printer, plus Wi-Fi or good mobile reception for the devices at intake and cash desk.

### Set rules for sellers

Clear rules save discussions on the bazaar day. Put them in the bazaar description:

- **What is accepted:** for example clean, seasonal clothes up to size 176, toys, books, strollers and car seats. What isn't accepted, such as underwear or damaged items.
- **How many items** a seller may hand in. Many bazaars limit the number per seller so that intake is manageable.
- **Commission:** the share of the sale price that stays with the bazaar – for example for the daycare fund or a good cause.
- **Times** for drop-off and pickup.
- **Items not picked up:** what happens to them after a deadline, for example a donation to a charity?

### Create the bazaar in BazarPro

When you create the bazaar, pick the kind **Children's bazaar (number bazaar)**. It presets suitable settings: small labels, photos optional, 50 items per seller and discounts. Then add the title, location, date, the description with your rules and times, the allowed categories and the **commission**. With 15 % commission and an item for €10, the seller gets €8.50 and €1.50 stays with the bazaar.

A few settings are especially useful for kids' bazaars:

- **Maximum number of sellers and items per seller:** Limit the number of sellers and their items so that intake and space are enough.
- **Allow items without photo:** Nobody likes photographing 40 bodysuits. Without photos, creating items is much faster.
- **Small labels:** 40 per A4 page with cutting lines. Each shows the QR code, title, **seller number** and size – like at a classic number bazaar.
- **Intake fee (optional):** Many bazaars charge a small amount per item on top of the commission, e.g. €0.20. It is deducted from the payout at settlement.
- **Hand-in and pickup:** Enter the times. BazarPro then sends sellers an email with all details the evening before and reminds them to pick up after the bazaar.
- **Access code (optional):** If the bazaar should first be open only to parents of your daycare, give the code out only there.
- **Categories:** If a category name contains “Kleid”, “Jacke”, “Hose”, “Mode” or “Shirt” – such as “Kinderkleidung” or “Jacken & Hosen” –, BazarPro asks for **size** and **brand** when an item is created. For “Schuhe” it asks for the shoe size.
- **Locations:** Create areas like “Size 50–74”, “Size 80–98”, “Toys” or “Books”. At intake you assign each item to an area – buyers see where it is when they scan it.

The step-by-step guide is in [Create a bazaar](/en/veranstalter/basar-anlegen/).

### Choose features

In the **How are items sold?** step you decide how items are sold:

| Feature | At a kids' bazaar |
| --- | --- |
| **Discount items** | Popular for the last hour: from a set time, sellers may lower their prices, up to your maximum discount. |
| **Questions to the seller** | Mainly worthwhile for bigger items like strollers, high chairs or car seats. |
| **Online price offers** | Also mostly for bigger items – nobody haggles over a €2 bodysuit. |
| **Negotiating on site** | Usually too much effort at the cash desk with many small items. |

### Café and presale

A **café** with coffee and homemade cake is part of almost every kids' bazaar – and often brings in a good share of the income for the good cause.

Many bazaars also open earlier for **pregnant women** with a companion so they can choose baby things in peace. Decide from when and put it in your advertising. Proof such as the maternity record is often shown at the entrance.

## Part 2: Sellers get ready

Sellers register for the bazaar through the link and create their items from home – with size, price and, if they like, a photo and description. With **Create & add another**, bazaar, category and condition stay set, so even a big pile of kids' clothes is entered quickly. With **Multiple selection** under “My items”, they print the labels of all items at once as a PDF.

The guide for sellers: [List an item](/en/verkaeufer/artikel-inserieren/).

Give sellers a few tips:

- **Attach labels firmly:** with a safety pin or thread, not just tape. For toys in a bag, attach the label to the bag.
- **Bundle what belongs together:** put the parts of a set, puzzle or toy in one bag and create **one** item for it.
- **Fair prices:** used kids' clothes sell best at a fraction of the new price. Anyone who prices too high takes a lot back home.
- Give **size and brand** – that's what parents look for.

<Aside title="Price on the label?">
  With discounts, there is **no price** on the label so that sellers can still lower it during the bazaar. Buyers see the current price when they scan the QR code with their phone – put up a sign at the bazaar saying so. If you don't use discounts, you can turn on **Print the price on the label**.
</Aside>

## Part 3: Intake and sorting

### What to have ready

- **Smartphones or tablets** for intake and cash desk – BazarPro runs in the browser. A **hand scanner** makes the cash desk even faster but isn't a must. Don't forget power banks.
- **A printer** with paper for missing labels.
- **Safety pins, tape, bags**.
- **Tables, clothes racks, boxes** and signs for the areas.
- **Change** and a lockable cash box.

### Assign helpers

In BazarPro you make sellers of your bazaar **helpers** with one click. Helpers can accept items, sell at the cash desk, settle up and print QR codes and invoices. They can't change settings or delete the bazaar.

Plan **more helpers for intake than for the cash desk**. At a kids' bazaar, intake with many small items is the most demanding station.

### Intake

Many kids' bazaars accept items the **day before** or early in the morning so that there's enough time to sort before the sale.

<Steps>

1. **A seller arrives with their items.** Scan their **digital ID** – BazarPro shows all registered items of that seller.

2. **Accept all items.** Check the items briefly and accept them all at once with **Accept all**. You can also scan and accept single items – for example if you want to assign them a location.

3. **Print missing labels.** If a seller forgot labels, print them right at intake.

4. **Items not accepted** go straight back – in BazarPro they simply stay “registered”.

</Steps>

Spontaneous sellers without registration are added in the seller management with **Add seller without account**, and their items are recorded in Item management with **Add item**. Whether you allow this at a kids' bazaar with a seller limit is up to you.

### Sorting

After intake, the items are sorted into areas: clothes by **size**, plus toys, books, gear and shoes. Well-labeled tables and clothes racks make it easy for buyers – and for your helpers when sorting back at the end.

## Part 4: The bazaar day

### The sale

- **Buyers browse and scan.** With their phone camera, they see a photo, size, condition and the current price of every item – without an app.
- **The cash desk scans.** On the **Cash desk** page, a helper scans all items of a family into one cart, takes the total and completes the sale once. Every seller gets an email and sees the sale under “My sales”.
- **Discounts in the afternoon.** If you allow discounts, sellers lower their prices from the set time. The label stays the same.
- **Receipt on request.** Buyers can give their email address at the cash desk and get **one receipt** for their whole purchase.

<Aside type="caution" title="Plan enough cash desks">
  Even with a cart, every label has to be scanned once. With families carrying full bags, that takes time. Better plan one more cash desk – BazarPro runs on any smartphone, you don't need extra checkout hardware. One helper can lay out the items label side up while a second one scans.
</Aside>

### Return and payout

After the sale, the remaining items are **sorted by seller** – thanks to the seller number on every label, this works like at a classic number bazaar. BazarPro shows you for each seller which items sold and which go back – so you know exactly what to look for.

<Steps>

1. **The seller shows their digital ID.** You scan it and see all their items.

2. **Return unsold items.** BazarPro lists the items that are still there.

3. **Pay out.** The sales minus commission and intake fee are already calculated.

4. **Finish the settlement.** One click confirms both. Afterwards, this seller's sales and returns are final.

</Steps>

## Part 5: After the bazaar

### Leftovers and donations

Not everyone picks up their items. In Item management you see which items are still **Available**. Clarify them with the sellers or donate them after the deadline you set in your rules – to a clothing bank, a women's shelter or a family center.

### Cash count

In the **Cash count** you see the day's sales, the commission as income, the payouts to sellers and what's still open – and you can compare it with the cash. Settle the café's income separately.

## Spreadsheet or bazaar software?

Many bazaars start with a **spreadsheet**: every seller gets a number and a list, every item a label with seller number, item number and price. At the cash desk, everything is typed in, and in the evening it's added up per seller.

That works – as long as nothing goes wrong. Typos at the cash desk, illegible labels and adding up in the evening cost a lot of time. With bazaar software like BazarPro:

- sellers create their items **themselves**, you don't have to type up lists,
- the cash desk **scans instead of typing**,
- the **settlement per seller** is ready immediately,
- sellers get their **seller number** automatically, and it's on every label,
- sellers can reuse their unsold items at the **next bazaar**.
